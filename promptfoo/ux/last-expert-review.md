## libe·bob — expert audit (joiner with one prediction)

The page demonstrates strong mobile-first UX fundamentals with clear visual hierarchy, accessible tap targets, and Spanish-language microcopy. However, there are notable gaps in member control, system feedback, and conversion optimization that would frustrate a returning user like Bob. Specifically: (1) Bob's identity is marked ('vos') but his member actions are limited—no visible 'leave group' or 'switch group' option; (2) the prediction CTA is duplicated (web + WhatsApp) without explaining when to use which; (3) the roster shows 'Sin puntos todavía' but doesn't explain *when* points appear or what Bob should do next; (4) no visible way to see Bob's own prediction history or compare his scores to others; (5) the 'Invitar amigos' button uses WhatsApp share but doesn't pre-fill the group name, forcing manual copy-paste; (6) no error recovery UI if a prediction fails to save. The design excels at Nielsen's 'aesthetic minimalism' and 'match to real world' (sports language, WhatsApp integration), but stumbles on 'user control' (member can't manage group membership), 'visibility of system status' (no feedback on prediction submission), and 'flexibility & efficiency' (no shortcuts for power users). For a returning member in a live competition, these gaps create friction.

## libe·anon — expert audit (anonymous visitor)

The page demonstrates strong mobile-first UX fundamentals but has critical conversion funnel gaps that would cause anonymous visitor bounce. Strengths: clear value prop (group name + player count), prominent WhatsApp CTA (44px+ pill, lime accent, frictionless wa.me link), semantic structure, Spanish microcopy, privacy-safe (no prediction data exposed). Critical issues: (1) The hero section buries the actual game mechanic—'próximos partidos' shows match times but zero explanation of 'what do I do with these?' until the scoring rules card at bottom. An anonymous visitor sees 6 matches and has no affordance to predict. (2) The join CTA ('Sumate · código P_LIBE_MTXMRMGW') is a WhatsApp deeplink, which is correct for this product, but there's no secondary path for someone who wants to preview/understand before committing to WhatsApp. (3) The roster (Alice, Bob, Carol) is trust-positive but lacks context—no avatars with initials, no 'joined 2 days ago' timestamps, no 'Alice is the admin' signal. (4) 'Sin puntos todavía' messaging is honest but creates perception of emptiness; could show 'First match resolves May 27 at 19:00' to set expectations. (5) Tap target audit: topbar buttons are 42px (below 44px minimum), though padding may compensate. (6) No error recovery path if the WhatsApp link fails or user closes the chat—no fallback email invite or manual code entry. (7) Microcopy 'Sumate' is colloquial (good for LA audience) but 'código' assumes familiarity with the join flow. (8) The 'compartí cómo va la penca' link at bottom is a ghost link pointing to /compartir/ which likely 404s for anonymous (no auth context). Accessibility: color contrast on .mantia-ink-soft (var(--ink-soft): #595851 on lime #c5f24e) is ~4.2:1, borderline WCAG AA. No alt text on SVG icons (aria-hidden is correct, but 'Inicio' label is present). Overall: this is a well-designed *retention* page for existing members, but the *acquisition* funnel for cold traffic is leaky. An anonymous visitor lands, sees a group of 3 people with no scores yet, and the only action is 'message this WhatsApp number with a code'—no way to preview, no social proof beyond names, no 'why should I trust this?'

## libe-comp·alice — expert audit (competition page personalized)

The page prioritizes discovery (upcoming matches) and group navigation over personalized status, which is misaligned for a returning member like Alice. After the header, the visual hierarchy flows: (1) competition nav chips, (2) 'mis pencas' section (good), (3) 'ranking global' (empty state, low value), (4) 'próximos partidos' (largest section, dominates fold). For Alice—who has already created a penca, made predictions, and is checking in—the page should lead with her group's leaderboard or her prediction status, not with a 6-match fixture list. The 'ranking global' section appears *before* match details but is empty, creating a dead zone. The CTA at the bottom ('Crear tu propia penca') is redundant—she already has one. The page reads as 'new user discovery' rather than 'returning player dashboard.' A returning member wants: (a) quick view of her group's standings, (b) confirmation her predictions are saved, (c) live match updates. Instead, she gets marketing-first structure. The 'mis pencas' row is well-placed but undersized (single row, no stats preview). The match list is thorough but should be secondary to personalized group context. This violates Nielsen's 'visibility of system status' (where am I in the competition?) and 'match to real world' (returning players expect a dashboard, not a fixture browser).

## libe-comp·anon — expert audit (competition page anonymous)

The page demonstrates strong mobile-first UX fundamentals but has critical gaps in conversion clarity and social proof effectiveness. Strengths: clear hero (Libertadores fecha 6), excellent visual hierarchy with Archivo Black display type, proper tap targets (42px buttons, 54px pill), semantic HTML structure, and Spanish microcopy. Weaknesses: (1) The dual-path CTA is ambiguous—only one button ('Crear tu propia penca') is visible; joining existing pencas requires scrolling to find 'código' mention in activity hint, violating mobile-first prominence. (2) Social proof ('1 penca activa · 3 jugadores') is buried in a gray activity-hint section below the fold, not elevated as a trust signal near the hero. (3) No visual distinction between 'create new' vs 'join existing' flows—a first-time user won't know both options exist without reading fine print. (4) The activity hint uses jargon ('penca activa') without onboarding context for cold visitors. (5) Match list below CTA creates scroll friction—the primary action isn't sticky or prominent enough. (6) No error recovery or help text for users who don't have a code. Accessibility is solid (semantic nav, color contrast ≥7:1 on lime bg), but the conversion funnel leaks at the critical 'which path?' decision. For an anonymous landing page, this prioritizes discovery (showing matches) over conversion (joining/creating). The page works well for returning users but underserves cold traffic.

## mun·bob — expert audit (NON-member viewing)

The page handles the identified-but-not-a-member edge case poorly. Bob (identified visitor, not in this penca) sees the same public roster + join CTA as an anonymous stranger would, with no acknowledgment that he's logged in or recognition of his existing Mantia identity. The UX misses a key conversion opportunity: 'You're in P_LIBE_MTX — join P_MUN_MTX too?' would be warmer and more persuasive than generic 'Sumate · código'. The page also lacks a 'Sign in' affordance or identity indicator (no avatar, name, or 'logged in as' badge anywhere), making it impossible for Bob to know he's authenticated. Privacy is safe (no member emails/phones leak), but the experience feels like a missed handshake. For a WhatsApp-native product where identity is the primary onboarding vector, this is a notable gap.
